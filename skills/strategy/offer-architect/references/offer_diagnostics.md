# Offer Diagnostic Scan

Use this reference when auditing an existing offer. Run this scan BEFORE deep questioning — show the user what you see first, then ask targeted follow-ups.

## Quick Diagnostic (First Pass)

When the user presents an existing offer, run through these 9 dimensions rapidly. For each, assign a preliminary score based on what you can infer:

- 🟢 **Strong** — Evidence of good design in this area
- 🟡 **Unclear** — Not enough information, or mixed signals
- 🔴 **Weak** — Clear problem or missing entirely

### Dimension 1: Market Hunger

**What to assess:** Is there proven demand? Are people actively seeking solutions?

**Red flags:**
- No prior sales at this price point
- User describes market as "anyone who..." (too broad)
- Solution looking for a problem (tech-first, not market-first)
- Market is "nice to have" not "must have"

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "How many people have paid you for this? At what price?"
- "What do your customers do TODAY to solve this problem? What's their current workaround?"
- "If your offer disappeared tomorrow, how would your customers feel? Annoyed or devastated?"

### Dimension 2: Customer Clarity

**What to assess:** Is the ideal customer specifically defined? Can you picture one real person?

**Red flags:**
- Target market described in demographics only ("SMBs", "millennials")
- No mention of psychographic triggers (fears, desires, frustrations)
- Multiple very different customer types served by one offer
- Customer's current alternative isn't named

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "Describe your best customer. What do they do? What were they struggling with before they found you?"
- "What made them buy? What was the trigger event?"
- "Who should NOT buy this? Who would be a bad fit?"

### Dimension 3: Promise Specificity

**What to assess:** Is the core promise specific, measurable, and believable?

**Red flags:**
- Vague outcomes: "grow your business," "improve performance," "get results"
- No timeframe for results
- No success metric defined
- Promise is about features, not transformation

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "In one sentence: what specific outcome does the customer get, by when?"
- "How would the customer know they got what they paid for? What would they measure?"
- "Is this promise something you've delivered before? How many times?"

### Dimension 4: Value Stack Architecture

**What to assess:** Does the value stack create overwhelming perceived value relative to price?

**Red flags:**
- Value stack is feature list, not outcome list
- Components don't clearly map to the core result
- No premiums or bonuses that amplify perceived value
- Components listed without perceived dollar values
- Stack includes items the customer doesn't value (but the creator does)

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "For each component: what specific result does it produce? Could the customer get this result without it?"
- "Which component is the 'Trojan Horse' — the one that by itself could justify the price?"
- "If you removed half the components, which half would the customer miss?"

### Dimension 5: Pricing & Economics

**What to assess:** Does the pricing work for both buyer and seller?

**Red flags:**
- Price doesn't match positioning (premium claim, low price)
- No cost-of-fulfillment estimate
- Negative or razor-thin margins
- Price based on cost-plus instead of value-based
- No clear relationship between price and result delivered

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "What does it cost you to deliver this offer to one customer? Include your time."
- "What's the margin? At your target volume, what's the monthly profit?"
- "If you doubled the price, would you lose more than half your customers? (If not, your price is too low.)"

### Dimension 6: Delivery & Fulfillment

**What to assess:** Can the offer be delivered reliably at target scale?

**Red flags:**
- Founder is the sole delivery mechanism
- No documented process or system
- Delivery quality degrades at volume (ask: "What happens with 10 clients? 50? 100?")
- DFY model with no team or automation plan
- Custom delivery for each client (no repeatable process)

**Scalability death points by model:**

| Model | Typical death point | What breaks |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| **DFY (founder delivers)** | 5-15 clients | Founder time, quality, burnout |
| **DFY (team delivers)** | 20-50 clients | Hiring, training, quality control |
| **DWY (group)** | 30-60 participants | Attention per participant, outcome quality |
| **DWY (1-on-1)** | 10-20 clients | Same as DFY founder |
| **DIY (course)** | 500-1000 students | Support volume, completion rates |
| **DIY (software)** | 1000+ users | Technical infrastructure, support |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "Walk me through what happens after someone buys. Step by step, who does what?"
- "What's your capacity? How many clients can you handle right now?"
- "What breaks first when you double your client count?"

### Dimension 7: Risk & Guarantee

**What to assess:** Does the guarantee match the delivery confidence and market type?

**Red flags:**
- No guarantee at all (for new/unproven offers)
- Unconditional guarantee on high-ticket (refund risk)
- Guarantee doesn't address customer's primary fear
- Guarantee is standard ("30-day money-back") without thought to what the customer actually fears

**Guarantee-market fit matrix:**

| Market type | Best guarantee type | Example |
|------------|-------------------|---------|
| B2B, measurable results | Conditional (tied to outcomes) | "Hit X metric or we work free until you do" |
| B2C, low-ticket | Unconditional | "Don't love it? Full refund, no questions" |
| B2C, high-ticket | Conditional with milestone | "Complete all modules and implement; if no result, full refund" |
| Commodity market | Unconditional + premium | "Full refund + keep the bonuses" |
| Monopoly/unique | No guarantee needed | The offer itself is proof |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "What's your current guarantee? Why that one?"
- "What's the customer's biggest fear about buying? Does your guarantee address it?"
- "What's your refund rate? If >10%, something in the offer or fulfillment is broken."

> For the full guarantee type taxonomy (10 types) and decision tree, see `references/offer_enhancers.md`.

### Dimension 8: Competitive Position

**What to assess:** Is this offer differentiated or commoditized?

**Red flags:**
- Can describe the offer using only generic category terms
- Competing primarily on price
- No unique mechanism or proprietary process
- Customer could get similar result from 5+ alternatives
- Positioning is "better" not "different"

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "What would a customer compare this to? What are the obvious alternatives?"
- "Why should they choose you over the alternative? And I don't mean features — I mean why YOU?"
- "Is there anything about your approach that nobody else does? A unique mechanism, method, or result?"

### Dimension 9: Traffic & Acquisition Readiness

**What to assess:** Is the offer designed for the traffic it needs to convert?

**Red flags:**
- Premium offer targeted at cold traffic (very hard)
- No social proof or case studies for cold audiences
- Offer relies on brand recognition it hasn't built
- No paid ads unit economics model
- Sales process requires high-touch for low-ticket offer

**Traffic-offer alignment:**

| Traffic type | What the offer needs |
|-------------|---------------------|
| **Warm (referrals, existing audience)** | Clear promise, social proof optional, guarantee optional |
| **Cool (pixel, lookalike)** | Specific promise, social proof needed, guarantee helpful |
| **Cold (interest-based ads)** | Ultra-specific promise, heavy proof, strong guarantee, risk reversal |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- "Where do your customers come from? Referrals? Content? Ads?"
- "If you had to acquire customers through cold Meta Ads tomorrow, would this offer convert? Why or why not?"
- "What social proof do you have? Testimonials? Case studies? Revenue numbers?"

## Common Diagnostic Patterns

These are recurring patterns you'll encounter. Recognize them quickly:

### The "Premium Label, Budget Price"
- **Pattern:** User claims premium positioning but prices at commodity level
- **Root cause:** Fear of charging more, or lack of proof that premium is justified
- **Fix:** Either increase price to match positioning (with added proof/guarantee) or reposition honestly as mid-market

### The "Founder Bottleneck"
- **Pattern:** DFY offer where founder does everything; wants to 10x revenue
- **Root cause:** No delivery system, no team, no automation
- **Fix:** Shift to DWY model, build team, or productize the methodology into DIY

### The "Everything Offer"
- **Pattern:** 15+ components, serves "everyone," solves "all problems"
- **Root cause:** Fear of niching, uncertainty about what actually drives value
- **Fix:** Identify the 2-3 core components, cut the rest, sharpen the promise

### The "No Proof" Launch
- **Pattern:** Brand new offer, never sold, launching to cold traffic with high price
- **Root cause:** Skipping market validation
- **Fix:** Sell to 5-10 warm contacts first. Get results. Get testimonials. Then scale.

### The "Invisible Differentiation"
- **Pattern:** Offer IS genuinely different, but positioning doesn't communicate it
- **Root cause:** Curse of knowledge — founder assumes market can see the difference
- **Fix:** Name the unique mechanism. Create a proprietary framework. Make the difference obvious in the offer name.

### The "Margin Trap"
- **Pattern:** Revenue looks good but fulfillment costs eat all profit
- **Root cause:** Didn't model costs before pricing, or scope creep in delivery
- **Fix:** Map every cost. Find the 80/20 — which 20% of delivery creates 80% of perceived value? Cut the rest.

### The "Ladder to Nowhere"
- **Pattern:** Multiple offers that don't logically connect or feed into each other
- **Root cause:** Offers built reactively rather than as a planned ecosystem
- **Fix:** Map the value ladder. Each tier should create the problem the next tier solves. If they don't connect, the ecosystem leaks revenue.
